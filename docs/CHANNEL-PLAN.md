# Channel plan

Platform rules and payout rates below are from general knowledge and change often.
**Check each platform's current terms before relying on any number here.**

## Positioning

- **Niche:** space and science, explained through scale and motion.
- **Promise to the viewer:** "one thing about the universe that changes how big you think it is, in under a minute."
- **Look:** dark space backgrounds, bodies drawn to true relative scale, big word-by-word captions, one calm narrator.
- **Why this niche:** broad international audience, advertiser-friendly, evergreen (videos keep getting found),
  and it suits what TRENDSTUDIO draws well. Timely hooks (launches, eclipses, discoveries) can be added on top.

Name ideas (check availability on every platform before choosing): Cosmic Scale, Scale of Space,
Orbit Minute, Tiny Blue Dot. Use the same handle everywhere.

## Series

| Series | Format | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Cosmic Scale** | zoom-out size comparisons | built: `src/series`, episode 1 done |
| **Born to…** | life story of one object (star, comet, galaxy) | one-off built (`star-life`); needs its own reusable format |
| **This Week in Space** | one timely fact tied to news | not built; needs a research step and a human fact-check |

Start with Cosmic Scale only. One recognisable series grows a channel faster than three half-series.

Both come from one episode spec: beats marked `only: "long"` appear only in the long cut.

## Posting

- **Rhythm:** 3 episodes a week to start. Consistency matters more than volume.
- **Upload natively** to each platform. Never post a link to another platform, and never upload a file with another platform's watermark.
- **Title:** the question, under 60 characters ("How big is the biggest star?").
- **Description:** one sentence, the sources, 3 to 5 hashtags (#space #science #astronomy plus one specific).
- **First frame:** already carries the hook caption; no separate thumbnail work for short-form.
- **Pin a comment** with a question ("Which star should be next?"). Replies help early reach.

## Money milestones

| Stage | Roughly | What unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | first 30 videos | nothing; this stage is about learning what holds attention |
| 1 | 1,000 subscribers + 10M Shorts views in 90 days (YouTube) | Shorts ad share: small, cents per 1,000 views |
| 2 | 10,000 followers + 100,000 views in 30 days (TikTok, eligible countries) | rewards on videos over a minute |
| 3 | long-form on YouTube (8 min compilations of a series) | the real ad income: dollars per 1,000 views |
| 4 | a few tens of thousands of followers | sponsorships, affiliate links |

The $50-per-video goal is realistic at stages 3 and 4, not before.

## What to measure

- **Viewed vs swiped away** in the first 3 seconds: the hook.
- **Average percentage viewed:** aim for 80%+ on a 35 s short. If it drops at one beat, cut or rewrite that beat.
- **Subscribers per 1,000 views:** whether the series makes people want more.
Change one thing per episode so you can tell what helped.

## Risks to manage

- **"Repetitive / mass-produced" demonetisation:** every episode needs its own topic, script and visuals. Do not publish near-duplicates.
- **Accuracy:** a wrong number in a science video gets called out in comments and hurts the channel. Fact-check every script.
- **AI disclosure:** the voice is synthetic. Platforms mostly require labels for realistic synthetic media; a stylised animation with an AI narrator generally does not need one, but check the current policy and label if in doubt.
- **Reused content:** everything here is original (drawn and synthesized by code), which keeps the channel clear of reuse claims.
